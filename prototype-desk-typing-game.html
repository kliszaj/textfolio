Approach

Display type at poster size, like the site's own ADRIAN headline. Mistakes show as outlined letters in pink; the timer is a five-colour bar along the bottom, one segment per case study.

Why it fits

The most striking, and the one that best survives the zoom: it reads even before the camera has fully landed.

Trade-off

You only see a few words ahead, which slows strong typists down; long words get wide.

Approach

Borrows the paper stack and the red correction mark already on the site: a ruled sheet, a red-pen caret, scribbled-out mistakes with the intended letter in your handwriting font.

Why it fits

The most personal: it uses the hand-drawn voice nothing else on the desk does, and the circled grade is a nice payoff.

Trade-off

The handwriting font is 2.6 MB, so it would need subsetting for this; busier than the others when you make lots of mistakes.

Approach

Diegetic: the camera zooms to an app window that already sits on the monitor's desktop (next to Slack, Figma and the rest), styled like the other fake apps.

Why it fits

Keeps the desk's fiction intact; the results graph gives the "how did I do" moment Monkeytype is known for.

Trade-off

Dark and small inside the window, so it reads less like your site; the zoom would need a new target (the window, not the browser).
